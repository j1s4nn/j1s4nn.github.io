JevGAG + DDAG — Regime-Aware Adaptive Temporal Granularity for Causal Video World Models: When Does Adaptivity Pay?

Latest 2026 · Preprint (Zenodo)

Preprint — DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23693517. A two-part research programme on adaptive temporal granularity for causal video generation and interactive video world models: Part 1 — JevGAG (the report) audits when a generator should spend fine per-frame compute and when a cheaper chunked mode is enough, and Part 2 — DDAG (the method) turns those findings into a deployable, regime-aware rule.

Findings: on a matched Tesla T4 and A100-80GB study of a causal diffusion backbone, the same mode pair sits on opposite sides of the cost break-even; hand-crafted context statistics cannot predict coarse-unsafe segments (AUROC 0.491), while an intrinsic probe on the generator's own prefill activations reaches AUROC 0.798 — yet it inverts to 0.143 on the other GPU, and only a multi-feature model over the probe plus a sampler convergence residual transfers across both (0.842 / 0.833). A training-free 25% latent blend cuts the switch-boundary seam by 25% and improves fidelity, and the regime map shows adaptivity pays only when the cheap mode is cheaper and worse — the programme's honest headline is that the cheap mode was never worse, so the optimal policy is a fixed mode and a router is only worth deploying in a precisely stated region.

Jev-Routed Temporal Granularity with Recovery and Hysteresis for Causal Video Generation

2026 · Preprint (Zenodo)

Preprint — DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23395414. Causal video generators emit a rollout one temporal chunk at a time, which invites spending fine per-frame computation only where a segment is hard. Realizing that idea requires three preconditions that are usually assumed rather than measured: a mode that is genuinely cheaper on the deployment hardware, a signal that predicts hard segments before generation, and an independent endpoint that certifies the trade.

Approach: audits all three preconditions end to end on Causal-rCM (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B-480p causal text-to-video diffusion), routing per segment among a frame-wise mode (c1-1), a chunk-3 mode (c3-3), and a near-zero-cost hold mode with a two-threshold controller that adds hysteresis and forced recovery. Routing is driven by the text-only Jev System One oracle, which answers typed difficulty, drift-risk, and motion-class questions before a segment is generated. Audits run on a 16 GB Tesla T4 and an A100-SXM4-80GB.

Findings: cost is hardware-conditional — a coarse forward is 3.01× a fine forward on the T4 against a 2.33× break-even (so coarse is never cheaper), but on the A100 the ratio falls to 1.60–1.75 and coarse is 1.46× faster in DiT time; a preregistered hand-crafted signal fails (AUROC 0.491) while the text-only Jev oracle reaches AUROC 0.788 [0.575, 0.946] at 0.67 s per decision; hysteresis and recovery cut switches (0.50→0.38 on the T4; 0.92→0.50 under injected noise on the A100) without worsening regret; and on 24 fresh A100 prompts a single-decision Jev policy saves 1.78 s of DiT time and 7.39 s of total time at a small −0.020 CLIP subject-consistency cost — a trade-off, not dominance.

wmdrift — Diagnostics for Video World Models

2026 · Research tool

Problem: long-horizon failures of autoregressive video world models — camera drift, temporal jitter, fading spatial memory, quality decay — are described qualitatively, not measured, so runs cannot be compared.

Approach: inference-only metrics combining ORB visual odometry with Umeyama Sim(3) alignment, detrended optical-flow spectral analysis, loop-closure LPIPS, and Laplacian-variance quality trends with optional CLIP alignment. Validated against known ground-truth poses on synthetic fixtures; 24-test suite; runs on CPU.

ARIA — Resilient LLM-Controlled Drone Autopilot

2026 · Multi-agent system

Problem: autonomous drones must survive actuator failure, accept natural-language commands, respect social constraints, and manage energy — usually solved in isolation.

Approach: four cooperating agents around a shared 6-DOF quadrotor model — self-healing control (PPO), an LLM pilot (Mistral-7B via llama.cpp), ethical guardrails (CLIP), and a digital twin forecasting energy and wind 60 s ahead (Kalman filter). One command regenerates every figure, telemetry log, and report; 19 unit tests; CI on GitHub Actions.

FER_2026 — Facial Expression Recognition: A Controlled 7-Model Study

2026 · Comparative study

Problem: it is unclear how much deep architectures buy over classical baselines when the evaluation protocol is not held constant.

Approach: a strictly identical protocol — same balanced CK+ corpus (2,450 images), same stratified split, same training loop — across KNN, SVM, a from-scratch CNN, VGG16, MobileNetV2, ResNet50, and EfficientNetB0. Results so far: ResNet50 100%, custom CNN 96.3%, EfficientNetB0 93.9%, SVM 73.1%, KNN 49.8% test accuracy.

Deep Learning for LTI System Identification (Z-Transform)

2025 · Course research (DIP)

Problem: can discrete-time filter identification be cast as learning over Z-transform pole-zero geometry, bridging classical DSP and deep learning?

Approach: pole-zero maps rendered as 64×64 images paired with 128-dim frequency-response vectors, comparing PoleZeroCNN, FreqMLP, and late-fusion FusionNet on 5-class filter classification — 99.7–100% accuracy on 750 held-out samples, with a complete 1,257-line LaTeX paper and 19 auto-generated figures.
